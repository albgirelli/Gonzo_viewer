The Gonzo dataset: lightweight subset

Dataset & viewerData downloads

A reduced version of the Gonzo dataset (about 50 MB) for teaching, quick exploration and modelling: region tables, downsampled 3D volumes and a finite element mesh,. No FreeSurfer or NIfTI tools needed. For research, use the original data on Zenodo.

Files

All concentrations are in mmol/L (= mM), amounts in µmol, times in hours after injection. The pre-injection scan is placed at t = 0, where the concentration is zero by definition.

Labels

Brain tissue uses FreeSurfer aparc+aseg numbers: cortex 1001–1035 (left) and 2001–2035 (right, Desikan–Killiany atlas), white matter 2/41, and so on. A CSF voxel has label 10000 + n, where n is the brain region it is closest to (so 14 → 3rd ventricle, 1028 → next to the left superior frontal cortex). 9999 is other intracranial tissue (dura, large vessels, unlabelled). Names are in regions.csv.

Loading in Python

import numpy as np, pandas as pd
regions = pd.read_csv("data/regions.csv")
vol = np.load("data/volumes/gonzo_2mm.npz")
c = vol["concentration"]          # (5, nx, ny, nz), float16, mmol/L
labels = vol["labels"]            # (nx, ny, nz)
cortex = (labels >= 1000) & (labels < 3000)
print(np.nanmean(c[:, cortex].astype(float), axis=1))

MATLAB or Julia users: .npz files are zip archives of .npy arrays (e.g. NPZ.jl, or readNPY for MATLAB); the CSV files work everywhere.

How the data were made

T1 relaxation times were mapped with Look-Locker and mixed inversion-recovery/spin-echo sequences, registered to the baseline anatomy, and converted to concentration with c = (1/T₁ − 1/T₁,baseline) / r₁, where r₁ = 3.2 L mmol⁻¹ s⁻¹ is the relaxivity of gadobutrol. The original 0.5 mm data were cropped to the head and averaged into 1 mm and 2 mm blocks. Region statistics were computed at full resolution. The script that builds this subset is in scripts/build_subset.py.

Diffusion tensor imaging (DTI) was acquired once, before the injection. The diffusion tensor D (a symmetric 3 × 3 matrix per voxel) is given in brain tissue only, NaN elsewhere. Mean diffusivity is MD = trace(D)/3, fractional anisotropy FA = √(3/2)·|λ − MD|/|λ| from the eigenvalues λ, and the principal direction is the eigenvector of the largest eigenvalue. In the downsampled volumes the tensor is averaged over each block before MD, FA and direction are computed, so FA is somewhat lower than at full resolution. The tensor can be used directly as an anisotropic diffusion coefficient (it describes water; the tracer, a larger molecule, diffuses about 7 times more slowly).

Source: Riseth et al., Gonzo dataset (CC BY 4.0), processing pipeline at github.com/jorgenriseth/gonzo.
